Pomeranian

The Pomeranian is a small companion dog with a thick, fluffy double coat and a fox-like face. The breed descends from much larger Spitz-type sled dogs from the Pomerania region, which is now part of Poland and Germany. Over generations the Pomeranian was bred down in size, and today it weighs only 3 to 7 pounds and stands 6 to 7 inches tall.

Queen Victoria helped make the small Pomeranian famous in the late 1800s. She owned several Pomeranians and exhibited them at dog shows, and her enthusiasm for the smaller size drove the breed's popularity across Britain and beyond.

The Pomeranian has a double coat made up of a soft, dense undercoat and a longer, straight outer coat. The coat comes in many colors, including orange, cream, black, sable, and white. A Pomeranian needs brushing several times a week to prevent mats, and it sheds heavily once or twice a year.

Pomeranians are lively, bold, and outgoing. They are alert watchdogs and tend to bark a lot, so training them early to stay quiet on cue is worthwhile. Despite their size, a Pomeranian often acts as though it were a much larger dog and may challenge dogs many times its size.

Two Pomeranians are known to have survived the sinking of the Titanic in 1912. They were carried into lifeboats along with their owners, and are among the best-known dog survivors of the disaster.

Pomeranians need moderate exercise. A daily walk and some playtime are usually enough. They are intelligent and eager to please, so they learn tricks quickly and do well with positive reinforcement training.

Common health concerns for the Pomeranian include a collapsing trachea, patellar luxation, dental problems, and a coat condition called alopecia X, which causes hair loss. Pomeranians usually live 12 to 16 years.
