Chihuahua

The Chihuahua is the smallest dog breed in the world. It takes its name from the Mexican state of Chihuahua, where the breed was first popularized in the 1800s. Most Chihuahuas weigh between 2 and 6 pounds and stand 5 to 8 inches tall at the shoulder.

Chihuahuas have a lifespan of 14 to 16 years, and some live to 20. They are one of the longest-lived dog breeds, which makes them a long-term commitment for their owners.

Chihuahuas come in two coat types: smooth coat and long coat. The smooth coat is short and glossy, while the long coat is soft and may be flat or slightly wavy. Both types shed moderately and need only occasional brushing. Chihuahuas come in nearly every color and pattern.

The temperament of the Chihuahua is bold, loyal, and often described as "big dog in a small body." Chihuahuas tend to bond very strongly with one person and can be wary of strangers or other dogs. Early socialization helps prevent excessive barking and snappiness.

Chihuahuas feel the cold easily because of their tiny size and low body fat. Many owners dress their Chihuahua in a sweater or coat in cool weather. They also enjoy burrowing under blankets to stay warm.

Chihuahuas need only modest exercise. A couple of short daily walks and some indoor play are usually enough. Because they are so small, they can be injured by rough play or a fall from furniture, so supervision around children and stairs is important.

Common health concerns for the Chihuahua include dental disease, patellar luxation (a slipping kneecap), and low blood sugar, especially in puppies. Some Chihuahuas are born with a molera, a soft spot on the top of the skull similar to a human baby's fontanelle. Brushing their teeth regularly and regular vet checkups help keep a Chihuahua healthy.
