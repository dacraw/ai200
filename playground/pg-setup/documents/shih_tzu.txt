Shih Tzu

The Shih Tzu is a small companion dog with a long, flowing coat and a sweet, flat-faced expression. The name Shih Tzu means "lion dog" in Chinese, a reference to the lions in Chinese Buddhist tradition and the breed's mane-like face fur. The breed is thought to have originated in Tibet and was kept and refined by the Chinese imperial court, where it was prized as a palace pet.

A Shih Tzu typically weighs 9 to 16 pounds and stands 8 to 11 inches tall. It has a sturdy, compact body, a short muzzle, large dark eyes, and a plume-like tail that curls over the back. Genetic studies have placed the Shih Tzu among the oldest dog breeds.

The Shih Tzu has a long double coat that comes in many colors, including gold, white, black, brindle, and liver. The coat needs daily brushing to prevent tangles, and many owners choose a shorter "puppy cut" to make grooming easier. Shih Tzus shed very little, which some people with mild allergies find helpful, although no dog is completely hypoallergenic.

The temperament of the Shih Tzu is affectionate, outgoing, and friendly. Unlike many small breeds, it was bred purely as a companion and not for hunting or guarding, so it generally gets along well with children, strangers, and other pets. Shih Tzus want to be close to their people and do not like being left alone for long periods.

Because of its short muzzle, the Shih Tzu is a brachycephalic breed, which means it can have trouble breathing and is sensitive to heat. It should not be exercised hard in hot or humid weather. Its large, prominent eyes are also prone to irritation and injury, so eye care is important.

Shih Tzus need only light to moderate exercise, such as short daily walks and indoor play. They usually live 10 to 16 years. Other common health concerns include dental problems, ear infections, and hip and knee issues.
