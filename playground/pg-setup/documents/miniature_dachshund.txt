Miniature Dachshund

The Miniature Dachshund is the smaller version of the German hunting breed known for its long body and short legs. The name Dachshund means "badger dog" in German, because the breed was developed to follow badgers into their underground burrows. The miniature variety weighs 11 pounds or less and was bred to hunt smaller prey such as rabbits.

Miniature Dachshunds come in three coat types: smooth, wirehaired, and longhaired. The smooth coat is short and shiny, the wirehaired coat is coarse with a beard and eyebrows, and the longhaired coat is soft with feathering on the ears and legs. Smooth coats need very little grooming, while wirehaired and longhaired coats need regular brushing.

The temperament of the Miniature Dachshund is curious, brave, and often stubborn. Dachshunds were bred to work independently underground, so they can be hard to train and may ignore commands that do not interest them. Patient, reward-based training works best.

Miniature Dachshunds have a surprisingly loud, deep bark for their size, which is a legacy of hunters needing to locate them underground. They also love to dig and burrow, and many will tunnel under blankets, pillows, and couch cushions.

The Dachshund's long spine makes it prone to intervertebral disc disease, which is one of the breed's most serious health risks. Owners can lower the risk by keeping their Dachshund at a healthy weight, discouraging jumping on and off furniture, and using ramps or steps. Obesity puts extra strain on the back, so careful feeding matters.

A Dachshund named Waldi was the first official mascot of an Olympic Games, at the 1972 Summer Olympics in Munich. Dachshund races, sometimes called wiener dog races, are also a popular event at fairs and festivals.

Miniature Dachshunds usually live 12 to 16 years. They need moderate exercise, such as a daily walk and playtime, and they are affectionate, loyal companions.
